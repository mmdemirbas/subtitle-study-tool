# A review of the whole repository, and what it changed

5 September 2026, extended 6 September. Every subproject was read, and what
could be run was run: the
browser extension's four suites, the daemon's, the alignment bench's two
offline entry points, and the shell scripts each of the small tools tells you
to start with. Fourteen changes came out of it. This says what was wrong, how
each was established, and which of the things found were deliberately left
alone.

The one to know about before anything else: **the focus box was being drawn
underneath the control panel**, which is the answer to "where is the fixed
focus study area, I think it is broken or invisible somehow". It was not
broken. It was behind the window you switch it on from.

## What was looked at

| Area | How it was checked |
|---|---|
| `browser-extension/` | 269 harness cases at 1x and 2x, 28 fallback, 5 options, 81 worker, 22 frames; surfaces driven through the service worker and photographed; placeholders measured against their boxes |
| `subtitle-daemon/` | 380 tests, ten of them written here; the HTTP boundary exercised over a real socket |
| `srt-translator/`, `subgen/`, `srt-viewer/`, `scripts/` | documented invocation attempted on each; the file server's bind confirmed with `lsof` |
| `bench/align/` | `shapes.mjs` and `regress.mjs` run against the local corpus |
| `docs/`, every README, both `CLAUDE.md` | claims checked against the code they describe |

Two areas were read but not executed: `scripts/setup-whisper.sh`, which clones
and builds whisper.cpp and fetches multi-gigabyte weights, and `bench/align`'s
`run.mjs`, which takes about twenty minutes. Neither is quoted below.

## What was wrong, and what it cost

### The focus box was inside the control panel

Both windows park in the top-right corner. Measured with both on screen at
1400x900: the panel occupied 1042,52 to 1384,559 and the box 1068,104 to
1388,301. The box is inside the panel on all four sides but four pixels, so
all five of its buttons were covered and the rare words it exists to show were
behind whatever the panel was showing. Study mode is switched on from the
panel, so that is the state it first appears in.

The box parks on the left now, level with the panel. Stacking cannot settle
two windows that want the same rectangle.

There was a second defect underneath it. Both surfaces sit in the top layer,
which paints in the order its entries were shown in, and every pointer move
over the film re-shows them both - panel first. So a panel dragged over the box
covered it until the mouse moved and lost the overlap from then on. Measured
with `elementsFromPoint` at the box's centre before the change: the panel
answered while nothing had moved, the box answered afterwards. The order is
reversed, and the harness has a case that drags one onto the other and asks
what is in front after three pointer moves.

### A phrasal verb was invisible in the tense the line used

The phrase index is built from each head verb's surface forms, and the rule for
`-ing` and `-ed` never doubled a final consonant. It held `puting`, `stoped`
and `planing`, which appear in no subtitle. "She is putting up with it" carried
no phrase at all, and "put up with" is the example the whole feature is written
around. The same rule dropped the e from every verb ending in one, so `lie`,
`die`, `see` and `agree` were indexed as `liing`, `diing`, `seing`, `agreing`.

Separately, the longest phrase did not reliably win: the tie-break on span sat
beside the rule on particle count rather than inside its equal case, so "stand
in for" was found and then replaced by "stand in". Which of the two came out
depended on the order the table happened to list them in. Ran, both orders,
before the change: "he stood in for me" marked "stand in" when the longer
phrase was listed first.

### The daemon acted on three bodies it should have refused

**`{"file_id": true}`.** `bool` is a subclass of `int` in Python, so the check
that the value was an integer admitted it, and the value then names the file.
Reproduced against a scratch cache: it wrote `True.srt` and `True.json`, after
which `list_subtitles` raised `ValueError` on `int("True")` and every endpoint
that lists what is held answered with a traceback. The directory stayed that
way until the two files were deleted by hand.

**`Content-Length: -1`.** Only the ceiling was checked, and -1 is under every
ceiling there is, so it reached `rfile.read(-1)` - which reads until the peer
closes rather than until the body ends. One request held a worker thread for as
long as the caller kept the socket open.

**A language code with a slash in it.** The word being looked up was quoted
before it became a filename; the `lang` and `to` beside it were not, though all
three arrive on the query string.

### A third of the cache could not be imported

An import carries a whole subtitle file, base64-encoded, against the 64KB
ceiling every other endpoint shares. Measured over the 273 files held on this
machine: the median encodes to 56KB, the largest to 200KB, and 96 of them were
answered with "body too large". Refusing an import means the extension pays a
second download for a file the daemon could have been handed - the one thing
the cache exists to prevent.

### Two different searches could share one cache entry

The search cache key was the readable name and nothing else. Every character
outside `a-z0-9` becomes an underscore, so a title written in a script with
none of them collapses to nothing: `君の名は` and `千と千尋の神隠し` both keyed as
`_en_none_none_none_none`, and the second search was answered out of the first
film's entry for the six hours it lived. The name was also cut at 120
characters, so two long titles that begin alike met in the same place. Turkish
and French titles keep their Latin letters and lose only the accented ones, so
they are diminished rather than merged.

Searches cost no quota, so this was never paid for in downloads. It was paid
for by auto-attach choosing a subtitle for a different film.

### A refusal looked exactly like an outage

`_gloss` returned a row of empty strings both when the model declined a word
and when it could not be reached, so its two callers had to guess, and both
guessed the same wrong way. The batch loop read an empty batch as a timeout and
stopped, handing every remaining word of the film to the tier below - but the
prompt asks for `""` on a proper noun, so a batch that happens to hold only
names comes back entirely blank. A cast list said early in a film is exactly
that batch. The single-word path did the same thing on a smaller scale: one
refusal put the model to sleep for sixty seconds.

What did not change: a word the model refused is still not passed down to
Google. That is a decision with a test of its own, and the test is right -
Google has no idea it is looking at a name, and "Paige" comes back from it as
"sayfa-numarası-yap".

### Smaller, and each one measured

- **The subtitle server was published to the network.** `run-http-server-here.sh`
  called `python3 -m http.server` with a port and no host, which is a wildcard
  bind. Confirmed with `lsof` before and after: it now reports `127.0.0.1:8080`
  rather than `*:8080`.
- **`config.example.json` could not be parsed.** The README's first instruction
  is to copy it. Its gloss comment quoted `"spare a minute"` with double quotes
  inside a double-quoted JSON string, so `json.load` stopped at line 15.
- **`subgen`'s setup script failed twice over.** It asked for
  `requirements.txt` while the file was named `requirements-legacy.txt`, and it
  passed `--no-binary=:all:`. Measured in a scratch venv: with that flag the
  resolve fails on the first line, because faster-whisper publishes no source
  distribution for the pinned 1.2.1; without it pip resolves all 24 packages.
- **The translator's test script named a path from before the repo migration**
  and passed the API key as a command-line argument, where `ps` can read it.
- **The cache search box showed "Search films, releases or l"** and then its own
  edge. It carried no styling, so the browser drew it 153px wide against a
  placeholder wanting 220, at every window size.
- **Cache writes were not atomic**, and a half-written search entry raised out
  of `/search` for every later search of the same title.
- **`gloss_many` dropped items that were not objects** rather than answering
  them, so every gloss after one sat under the word before it.

### A worker that never answered looked like a model with nothing to say

There are four ways a word ends up with nothing under it, and the marks record
exists to tell three of them apart. The fourth was invisible: every op lets a
failure reach the dispatcher, which answers `{transportError}`, and gloss alone
caught it first and returned an empty object. That arrives as a daemon which
was asked and had nothing to say, so the record blamed the model.

### The documents

The reports index listed 13 of the 17 write-ups, missing the four most recent.
The bake-off page said subgen's realtime engine uses a smaller model; there is
no realtime engine and subgen defaults to `large-v3`. The alignment bench
quoted a corpus that has since grown from 283 same-film pairs to 379. The test
README opened with "Fifty-five" cases against 267 registered, and the
paragraphs describing `fallback.html` sat under the `options.html` heading. The
file table in `browser-extension/CLAUDE.md` carried line counts roughly half
of today's. Both subgen spec documents read as current setup guides for a tool
that was never implemented; only the root README said otherwise, and neither
file is usually reached through it. `beyond-subtitles.md` pitched six Layer 1
ideas of which five have since shipped.

## Left alone on purpose

- **The `room` constant in the map clock.** Changing 58 to 50 is a no-op at the
  186px canvas the map actually draws at, because the tick ladder jumps
  15000 to 30000 there. Measured in the extension: 3, 4 and 4 marks either way.
- **Which frame draws the controls** when the player is a cross-origin iframe.
  Still the known gap; it needs a decision rather than a fix.
- **`Origin: null` in the allowlist.** A test pins it, and a `file://` page is
  the likely reason - the viewer opens that way. Worth deciding rather than
  removing.
- **The panel covering the focus box when dragged onto it.** That is the
  documented behaviour and it now holds; the two simply no longer start on top
  of each other.

## A second pass, 6 September

The first pass reviewed the whole repository. This one audited the parts of the
extension no dedicated read had covered: the aligner, and the five smaller
modules that decide what is fetched, what is looked up and what is kept.

### The deck lost what a reader had put in it, three ways

A storage read that failed answered as an empty deck, and both writers are
read-modify-write - so one failed read turned the next save into "the deck is
this one new word". With a deck of one entry and a single rejecting read, save
returned added:true and the deck came back holding only the new word, nothing
thrown and nothing logged. Remove wrote an empty list the same way.

The id was the clock in base 36 plus the word, so the same word saved against
two films inside one millisecond took the same id - and removal is by id. Both
saves came back as `mtpd832h-warrant` and deleting one card reported removed:2.

The export was tab-separated on the grounds that a tab-separated file has
nothing to quote. Anki reads it with Python's csv module, which honours quotes
whatever the delimiter is. On four entries, one holding a line of dialogue in
quotation marks, the file parsed as three rows rather than five, and an
unbalanced quote - ordinary when speech runs across two cues - swallowed its own
row and the two after it.

### The extension's search key had the fault the daemon's had

The daemon's key gained a digest earlier in this review and the extension's copy
did not, though the comment beside it says a search answered by one is answered
the same way by the other. So the two had quietly stopped agreeing, and this
side still collapsed any title with no Latin letters onto one key. Both now emit
the same string for the same search, and the parity suite diffs seven cases
rather than trusting the comment.

### A deletion the daemon refused was reported as done

`call` returns any answer carrying an `error`, whatever its HTTP status, which
is right where a refusal is part of the answer and wrong for a deletion. A
DELETE answered 500 resolved like a success: the file was recorded as deleted
everywhere and the queue entry that would have retried it was dropped, so the
next convergence copied the file back. Forgetting cached searches emptied that
queue too, though the daemon deletes no subtitles on that path.

### A fetch asked for a file this side was holding

The daemon does not know what the extension has, and the copy in that direction
is made on a down-to-up transition and gives up quietly when a push fails. A
file held here and never pushed was fetched through the daemon anyway - a
download out of five a day, spent on something already on disk.

### The aligner could answer with a number that is not one

`confidence` could be Infinity, and Infinity does not survive being written
down: the trace and the report both put an answer through JSON, where it becomes
null, and null against the accept threshold is false. An answer that had just
been applied read back as one that was refused. Nothing in the 6448-pair corpus
reaches it; the smallest construction that does is now in the harness.

The wide pass was also taken whenever it merely came back ok, though the comment
beside it says it is taken only when it beats the narrow pass. The window is in
the hypothesis count, so the same evidence scored over the wide window loses
0.92 decades. Over the corpus: 34 of 379 pairs took a wide answer where the
narrow pass had already said ok, all 34 scored lower, and none changed the shift
or the rate. The bench is unmoved by both changes - 59 better, 98 unchanged, 0
worse, and the by-kind medians identical.

### Found by the audits and left for a decision

Four in the aligner, each reproduced by the audit against constructed input and
none of them reached by the corpus:

- **A two-way tie is reported as "apply".** `windowOffset` refuses an ambiguous
  window; the top-level search has no equivalent, so an equally supported second
  peak is discarded silently. Over 40 seeds on a file whose cue blocks are
  duplicated at a small offset, the answer was a coin toss between two shifts
  four seconds apart, at the same confidence.
- **Confidence counts matches without asking where they sit.** Two ~500-cue
  files sharing one identical 100-cue block and otherwise unrelated came back
  "apply" at coverage 0.29, and all 500 cues were moved.
- **The answer depends on which file is passed first.** Six of 357 accepted
  corpus pairs disagree by more than 300ms when inverted; one is accepted one
  way and refused the other; one says "apply" both ways with the two answers
  499ms apart.
- **A rate correction is impossible under about 23 minutes.** The guard is a
  fixed 20-minute overlap, so 22-minute content can never receive one.

Each changes what the aligner accepts, so each is a product decision rather than
a defect to be quietly repaired. The bench is the place to settle them: it
already builds a truth from cue text and scores every method against it.

Also open, from the smaller modules: the language list changes depending on
whether the daemon is running, because the daemon's config answers over the
extension's setting; the dictionary cache keys an archive answer per line when
the daemon is merely reachable, which spends the free allowance faster than it
needs to; and the rarity table is not Unicode-normalised, so a decomposed
Turkish word reads as one the table has never seen, which is the rarest thing
there is.

## Open, and not acted on

Two readings that were not reproduced. Each is a mechanism read in the source,
not an occurrence observed, and each is written here rather than fixed because
guessing at the repair would cost more than the fault.

1. **The Find screen may empty itself under the reader.** `refresh` calls
   `forgetSearch` whenever `status.programme` differs from what was searched
   for, and the programme mark settles about 1.5 seconds after the duration
   arrives. A search run before that lands would be wiped a moment later. To
   check: open the panel and search while the video is still reporting `NaN`
   for its duration.
2. **An alternating now-playing announcement could thrash the attach.** The
   dedup on `warmedNext` keys on the last value only, so two values arriving
   in turn defeat it.

## State

Twenty-nine commits over the two passes, all on `main`, none pushed.

At the tip: 271 harness cases at 1x and at 2x, 33 fallback, 5 options, 84
worker, and 383 daemon tests in eight and a half minutes - all green.

The frames suite is the exception and it is not the code. It runs a nested
cross-origin player and waits up to twenty seconds for the overlay to appear;
its own comment records that this assertion failed on about one run in three at
eight seconds. It passed 22 of 22 twice this morning and fails one case now,
and the same failure reproduces at the commit that passed - so it is the
machine, which is carrying a one-minute load average of 357 from builds and
test runs in other projects. Re-run it when the machine is quiet.

Seven renders of the extension's surfaces are in
`browser-extension/tmp/shots/`, from before the second pass.
